I decided to study the impact of natural disasters on poverty through a data-driven, predictive approach focused on the Philippines. Rather than analyzing a single event such as Typhoon Haiyan (2013), I will build a comprehensive dataset covering multiple disasters—typhoons, floods, and earthquakes—occurring in the Philippines and Southeast Asia from 2000 to 2024. For each disaster, I will collect information on intensity, location, affected population, pre- and post-disaster poverty rates, and key economic indicators such as GDP growth and infrastructure losses.Using this dataset, I aim to develop predictive machine learning models that estimate how poverty levels evolve in the years following a disaster of given intensity. I will compare methods such as linear regression, random forest, and gradient boosting, training on older events and testing on recent ones, including Haiyan. This will allow me to quantify the relationship between disaster characteristics and poverty outcomes while evaluating the Philippines’ economic resilience to climate shocks. Ultimately, the project seeks to answer: To what extent can we predict post-disaster poverty trajectories based on disaster intensity and socioeconomic conditions? The goal is to inform data-driven, proactive poverty-reduction and recovery policies.
